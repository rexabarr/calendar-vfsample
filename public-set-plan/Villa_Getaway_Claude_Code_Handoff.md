## 1. Concept

A U.S.-run business selling fixed-price private getaway packages in the Dominican Republic (DR), aimed at American and Canadian travelers who want an international alternative to U.S. travel. The owner would pilot in the DR first.

**Business model:** an *experience operator* (tour-operator style), not a travel agency and not a resort. The operator buys the pieces (villa, chef, driver, staff) per booking, bundles them under one brand at one all-in price, and keeps the spread. No property owned, no standing staff. Positioning: *private concierge experience*, not "travel agency."

**Key differentiators vs. resorts:** privacy (no shared spaces), personalization, one all-in price (no resort fees), a point-of-contact, in-villa food, private transport, flexible timing (no shuttles/buses).

**Target customer (as described by the owner):** men 35–55, income $120K+, married or single, traveling alone or with a friend; Americans and Canadians; large and medium-size cities. Public Set targets groups of 3–4.

**Primary marketing channel:** Instagram, with content made by the hostess/host team (villa tours, welcome shots, food, views). See section 10 for platform rules.

## 3. Pricing Mechanics

- **Card processing:** 2.9% of list price + $0.30 fixed per transaction (placeholder; cross-border cards for Canadian customers can run higher, 5%+).
- **Public Set price formula:** `ROUNDUP((costs_before_processing + 0.30) / (1 − 0.029 − target_margin), −1)`, i.e., rounded up to the nearest $10 to hit a 36% target margin.
- **Villa and No Villa prices** were set to hit profit targets ($2,400 original target; later set to $7,490 / $6,080 for the Villa Package, and $1,800 / $1,400 targets for the No Villa Plan, then rounded).
- **Profit** = list price − costs before processing − card processing.

## 10. Marketing and Instagram

**Customer profile notes:** at $120K income, a $5,000–$7,500 trip is a stretch (about 5–6% of gross income), so the likeliest buyers earn $200K+ or are single guests treating it as a splurge. The shorter, cheaper packages are the entry offers. Canadians: provincial consumer rules, CASL (email consent), PIPEDA (privacy), and higher cross-border card fees.

**Channel plan:** Instagram content → DM/call → deposit and signed terms. Run the account under the business with a written content-ownership agreement with the hostess, to avoid single-person dependency. Disclose paid or commissioned promotion (#ad).

**Instagram/Meta policy (summarized from Meta's published pages; check current rules before finalizing):**
- Welcome shots, villa tours, food, views, and swimwear at the pool are generally fine; nudity and sexual activity are removed.
- Meta treats content as indirect solicitation when it shares contact info or suggests direct contact *and* includes a sexually suggestive element (sexual slang, sexualized emojis, suggestive poses).
- Prices or any sign of a transaction alongside solicitation are also flagged.
- Ads are stricter than organic posts (no content facilitating sexual encounters or discussing sexual practices).
- Content rules of thumb: frame the host as part of the scene, keep captions about the stay, avoid sexualized emojis/hashtags and words like "companion," don't pair prices with posts featuring her, moderate comments/DMs, and keep a website, email list, and backup channel.

**Claims on the site/IG:** list inclusions and exclusions explicitly (flights, excursions, tips excluded); show real photos and real guest reviews with permission; show total price, cancellation tiers, and the $400 date-change fee before checkout; describe staff by role (chef, driver, host/concierge). Avoid unproven superlatives, guarantees ("unforgettable"), "licensed/insured/safe" until documented, stock photos presented as the villa, fake scarcity, and any statements about what is legal in the DR.

**Guest-comfort feature (replaces an earlier "preview party" idea):** a **"Meet Your Host"** step — short profile videos of two or three hosts plus a quick video call with the matched host before booking. Included in the booking, or a $50–$100 fee to cover coordination; hosts are paid for the time. A live in-person line-up of eight hosts for guests to choose from was **not** adopted.

**Gratuities:** publish a simple tipping guideline for all staff (chef, driver, security, hostess/housekeeper).

## 11. Cancellation Policy (final tiers)

| Cancel before arrival | Refund |
|---|---|
| 30+ days | Full refund, minus the non-refundable booking fee |
| 17–29 days | 50% |
| Under 17 days | 20% |
| Under 7 days or no-show | 0% |

- **Booking fee:** flat **5% of the purchase price**, labeled a *non-refundable booking fee* (not a "processing fee," since actual card cost is lower).
- **Tier math:** refund percentages apply to the price *after* the booking fee is removed.
- **Date changes:** $400 rebooking fee, subject to availability.
- **Flights:** guest responsibility; no separate flight-cancellation fee (a flight cancellation is treated as a guest cancellation under the tiers). No refunds for airline delays, cancellations, or missed connections; airport pickup adjusted for delays where reasonable, with possible charges for changes that create real cost.
- **Insurance:** not offered for now. Recommend guests buy their own travel insurance.
- **Late-cancel retention:** at the under-17-day tier the operator keeps about 80% of the price, above expenses; some jurisdictions can challenge charging well above actual loss as a penalty, so have the attorney review.
- Align every vendor's cancellation terms (villa, chef, hostess, housekeeper, driver, security) with these tiers; consider a deposit at booking with the balance due at 30 days, and credits instead of refunds where possible.

**Draft policy language (for attorney review)**

> **Cancellation and Changes.** A non-refundable booking fee equal to 5% of the package price is retained on every cancellation. Refunds are calculated on the package price less the booking fee, based on the number of days between written notice of cancellation and the scheduled arrival date: 30 or more days, 100% refund; 17 to 29 days, 50% refund; fewer than 17 days, 20% refund; fewer than 7 days or failure to arrive, no refund. A guest may request a change of dates, subject to availability, for a rebooking fee of $400. Flights and other travel to and from the destination are not part of the package and are the guest's responsibility; no refund is provided for delayed, canceled, or missed flights or connections. We do not offer insurance and recommend that guests purchase their own travel insurance. By booking, the guest confirms they have read and accepted these terms. *[Attorney to review for enforceability and local-law requirements.]*

## 12. Legal and Structure

**Legality:** packaging and selling villa getaways is a legal business; the legal work is in setup. Questions for an attorney:
- **Seller-of-travel rules:** a few U.S. states (e.g., CA, FL, HI, WA) require registration, sometimes a bond or trust account; depends on where customers live.
- **Entity/liability:** LLC; written contractor agreements with each vendor; check classification of the independent-contractor hostess (directing her content/work can affect status).
- **Villa agreement:** owner must permit commercial-style short-term use; confirm insurance and local rules.
- **Security:** licensing for security providers (especially armed) in the DR.
- **Customer terms:** payment, cancellation/refunds, liability waiver, inclusions/exclusions.
- **Taxes:** lodging/sales tax on the package; local DR taxes on services delivered there.
- **Canada:** provincial consumer-protection rules, CASL, PIPEDA.

**Which lawyer:** hire a **U.S. business attorney first** (ideally travel/hospitality): LLC, seller-of-travel registration, customer terms, contractor agreements, insurance/tax structure; ask for a trusted DR referral. Then **DR local counsel** once villa and vendors are chosen: villa agreement and permitted use, security licensing, hiring local contractors (labor/tax), local taxes, dispute resolution. Start with a flat-fee consult and mention any pilot date.

**Insurance:** a "trip protection" box on a booking site is *real insurance sold through a licensed partner*, not insurance relabeled. Options later: (1) affiliate/API partnership with a licensed travel-insurance provider (insurer handles policy and claims; check commission rules by state); (2) own "flex" terms (paid better refund tier or free date change) — not insurance, but have the attorney check cancellation-waiver rules. Do not create an unlicensed pay-out product.

**Location:** DR is the pilot location because the cost structure (villa, chef, security, labor) works there; stateside would need much higher prices. Challenges: new-brand trust at this price, emergency backup (sick guest, vendor no-show, hurricane), overlapping U.S./local law, insurance coverage abroad. Suggested approach: pilot with a few friends-and-family or discounted bookings to test vendors, experience, and real costs before spending on marketing.
